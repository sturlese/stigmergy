Retrospectives now run within 48 hours of the incident closing

We have been running retrospectives whenever a calendar slot happened to be free, which in
practice meant one to three weeks after the incident. That is now changed: a retrospective runs
within 48 hours of the incident being closed, or it does not run at all and the incident is
recorded as unreviewed.

The problem with the old arrangement was not that people forgot. It was that by the time the
retrospective happened, everyone had already privately decided what the cause was, and the
meeting became a negotiation between settled positions rather than an investigation. The details
that would have changed anyone's mind — what the dashboard actually showed, what was tried
first, which alert nobody looked at — were gone.

Two options were on the table. The first was to keep the current scheduling and improve the
note-taking during incidents so the details survived. The second was to move the retrospective
close enough to the incident that the details had not yet decayed. The first was rejected because
it asks people to do careful documentation at the worst possible moment, and the evidence is that
they do not.

The 48-hour window is deliberately short enough to be inconvenient. A retrospective that is easy
to schedule is one that gets scheduled last.

The consequence is that some retrospectives will be attended by fewer people than before, and
that is accepted. A partial retrospective held while the details are fresh is worth more than a
complete one held after everyone has settled on a story. Recording an incident as unreviewed is
also a real outcome and not a failure to be worked around — it makes the cost visible instead of
hiding it behind a meeting that produced nothing.

This applies to every team. It is not tied to any one client, product or system, and it replaces
whatever local convention each team had before.
