Cofers Legal on the wording of the renewal clause

Cofers Legal came back on the renewal clause we asked them to look at, and their answer is worth
recording because it changes what we can offer rather than only how it is written.

Their reading is that the clause as drafted lets either side walk away at renewal without saying
why, and that we have been describing it internally as though notice were required. Both
readings fit the words, which is the problem: the clause has never been tested, and whichever
side tests it first gets to argue its own reading. They recommend replacing the sentence rather
than adding to it, because an addition would leave the ambiguous sentence in place beside the
correction.

They were careful to separate what they were asked and what they were not. They reviewed the
renewal clause only; nothing they said applies to the rest of the agreement, and they did not
look at the schedules at all. Anything beyond that needs its own scope letter.

The engagement itself finishes with this answer, so there is nobody retained to follow up unless
we ask for a new piece of work. That is the practical constraint on treating this as an open
thread.

No change has been made to the agreement yet. This records their reading and the limits they put
on it, so the commercial conversation starts from what was actually said.
