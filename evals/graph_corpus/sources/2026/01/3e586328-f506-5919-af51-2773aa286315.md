### Readable content

An internal memo written by Priya Raman, staff engineer responsible for the alerting pipeline, on 22 January 2026.

Today Tidewell raises an alert every time a probe crosses a temperature threshold. That is why Frostvale Distribution received 1,900 alerts in December. Most crossings are harmless: a door opens for four minutes at a supermarket dock and the air warms, then recovers.

What matters for product safety is not a single crossing but the cumulative time a shipment spends outside its temperature band. The memo proposes an excursion budget: the total minutes a shipment may spend out of range before the product is at risk. For frozen goods a common budget is 30 cumulative minutes above minus 15 degrees Celsius. For pharmaceuticals kept between 2 and 8 degrees, customers set the budget in their Good Distribution Practice procedures, often 60 cumulative minutes.

Instead of alerting on every crossing, Tidewell would alert when a shipment has consumed 50 percent and 80 percent of its budget, and escalate when it is exhausted. Coldpath would show a budget gauge per shipment.

Priya expects this to remove most of the noise Frostvale complains about while catching the slow, dangerous excursions that single thresholds miss. She recommends building it before the Altamira Pharma Logistics pilot, because pharma customers think in excursion budgets already.
